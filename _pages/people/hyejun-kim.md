Hyejun Kim is a Ph.D. student working where RNA biology meets computation.
Hyejun is currently at the School of Biological Sciences, Seoul National University,
studying how RNA sequence and structure together determine function,
with a focus on microRNA biogenesis. Hyejun is also interested in RNA stability and
translational control, and combines conventional NGS analysis
(RNA-seq, CLIP-seq, Ribo-seq) with Nanopore direct RNA sequencing
and machine learning.

Beyond this work, Hyejun took part in a 2025 *Nature Microbiology* study
showing that a long non-coding RNA of human cytomegalovirus counteracts
nuclear cGAS, a sensor of foreign DNA, to help the virus evade immunity.
Earlier, Hyejun led a study of microRNA profiles in seborrheic dermatitis
(*Scientific Reports*, 2022). Hyejun also writes small tools for
everyday transcriptome analysis, such as utilities for GENCODE annotations
and for building clean reference sets of mRNAs.

## Current project

**Codename HAIRPIN ORACLE.** Every primary microRNA folds into a hairpin,
and the Microprocessor (DROSHA–DGCR8) decides whether to cut it.
HAIRPIN ORACLE is a deep neural network that reads an RNA sequence
and predicts how efficiently that cut will happen, for any pri-miRNA,
including ones nobody has tested yet.
The goal is to turn high-throughput measurements into a map of the whole
sequence space, and to ask what the model learns about structure along the way.
